Pagefold swaps a container, but a page is more than that: its title, its description, the class of its body, what a screen reader should hear and where the focus goes. Three plugins take care of those. The panel under the page shows their work as it happens.

<Demo name="inspector" height={540} files={['main.js', 'index.html', 'style.css']} />

## What to try

- Click **About**. The title, the description and the body class change, and the panel reads `Navigated to: About us`.
- Click **Label**. The heading says one thing and its `aria-label` says another. The announcement uses the label.
- Click **Español**. The `lang` of the document changes to `es`, and so does everything else.
- Look at **focus is on**. After every visit it is the content, so a keyboard user starts at the top of the new page and not on a link that no longer exists.

## How it works

```js
new Pagefold({
  plugins: [
    new PagefoldHead(),      // title, meta tags, html lang
    new PagefoldBodyClass(), // the class of the body
    new PagefoldA11y(),      // announces the page, moves focus
  ],
})
```

- **[Head](/docs/head)** merges the `<head>` of the new page: it changes the tags that differ and leaves the rest alone.
- **[Body class](/docs/body-class)** copies the class of the new body, so styles that depend on it keep working.
- **[A11y](/docs/a11y)** says the name of the page, taken from the `aria-label` of the heading, the heading or the title, and moves focus to the content.
